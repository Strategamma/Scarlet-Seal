# Gameplay quality roadmap

Ratings are targets, not claims. Score playability, ease of use, fun, and reward separately after observing first-time players on phone and desktop.

## 7.5 — Clear enough to play without instruction

- Keep rules and secrets server-authoritative. Derive one turn phase from the game view; render choices from legal moves.
- Show each card's **value, name, effect, and deck count** clearly. Artwork must never cover rules text.
- Show current and next player, all seats and seal scores, last action, public discards, and recent public plays.
- Keep decisions in-page. Back returns one step; leaving asks for confirmation. Give each state change clear text and brief motion.
- Gate: five newcomers can start, make three legal turns, explain the last result, and leave/rejoin without help. No blocked controls or overlap at 320px, 390px, and desktop.

## 8.5 — Smooth, social, and satisfying

- Add a short guided first round with contextual rules only when needed, including public versus private information.
- Make action effects distinct but fast: target highlight, accurate result, elimination/protection response, and seal award. Keep reactions optional and compact.
- Improve recovery from disconnects, invalid choices, and fast bot turns; preserve a readable event timeline.
- Gate: most newcomers finish a round without asking what to do next; test a mixed human/bot room, reconnect, keyboard, touch, reduced motion, and screen reader announcements.

## 9.5 — Delight without slowing the table

- Tune animation timing, waiting states, round reveal, and match celebration from playtest evidence. Let repeat players move quickly.
- Offer optional strategic help: card-count reference and what public plays imply, without revealing hidden information.
- Explain why outcomes occurred and make rematches effortless, including after losses.
- Gate: repeated newcomer sessions show strong ratings in all four categories, low misclick and abandon rates, and no critical usability or rules defects.
